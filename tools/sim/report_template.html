Method and limits

The four arenas

Single-target DPS
One rooted, immortal dummy. The bot starts at its preferred range, so no walk-in is counted. 30 s.
Pack DPS
Six rooted immortal dummies inside a 24 px circle, damage summed. Rewards AoE. 30 s.
Clear speed
Packs of eight 60 HP fodder arrive from a random bearing, 130 px out, whenever the last pack is down to two. Kills per minute; the player is immune. The only offensive arena where on-kill procs fire. 40 s.
Survival
A brawl: the real enemy mix (fodder, swarmer, brute, caster) at real stats spawns on a ring around the player and hits back. The alive count grows by one every 5 s up to 30, and difficulty rises by 1.0 every 15 s through the same seam the run clock uses. The bot fights at its range without running away. The number is seconds until the first hit that would have killed it (the engine's own death-prevention hook catches it), capped at 180.

How the bot plays

It presses the real input actions. Aiming is the shipped auto-aim, so it aims no better than a controller player. Its play policy (button pattern, skill use, distance) is chosen per kit and per arena by search: 288 policies on a coarse grid (396 for the Spark, whose grid adds a familiar re-summon rotation and two Fireball holds), the best 8 re-run on 3 seeds, then the best of those carried into clear-speed and survival trials.

Rotations: L mash light · H mash heavy · L2H light to chain depth 2, then heavy · C@1.5 hold heavy 1.5 s (channel) · W@1.5 hold light · LS mash light, tapping heavy to re-summon the Spark's familiar whenever none is alive. Skills: q, e, qe on cooldown · qed adds dash on cooldown · e_once_q E once at the start (stances) plus Q on cooldown.

Reading a comparison

Every pick, mod and loadout is compared with the same kit without it, on the same seeds and the same candidate policies (common random numbers), and credited with its best policy. Each ratio carries a 95% interval from the kit's pooled run-to-run variance (estimated from every build of that kit at once). Blue and red mean the change survived a Benjamini-Hochberg correction at a 10% false-discovery rate across the whole table family; grey means it did not, even if the drawn interval misses zero.

Value, Offense and Survival columns rank on shrunk estimates (empirical Bayes, spike-and-slab prior fitted per kit and arena): a change that is mostly noise collapses toward zero, while a clearly significant one keeps most of its size. That stops a noisy survival swing from topping or sinking a table. The per-arena cells show the raw measurement and its interval.

Capstones and evolutions are measured on top of their prerequisites, against those prerequisites alone, so their number is what the capstone itself adds.

What this cannot tell you

Feel, readability, fun, animation timing and whether a telegraph is fair. Survival is a stand-and-fight test: the bot does not kite, because with kiting the result measured whether the bot happened to corner itself (the same build went down 0 or 5 times depending on the seed). So move speed and dash picks, whose value is escaping, are undervalued here. Immortal dummies never drop below full HP, so execute-style bonuses show up only in clear speed and survival.

Profile is clean: signature weapon, no passive tree, no trinkets, no workshop upgrades.
